Al Quoz is not a place you wander into. It is an industrial district with an arts
quarter inside it, the addresses make no sense, and nothing about the approach
suggests you are about to find twenty contemporary galleries behind a row of
warehouses.

So it rewards a plan. Here is the one we give people who ask at the bar.

## 8:30am — arrive before the parking goes

The single most useful thing in this entire guide.

Street parking on 17th Street is free and, before about nine, half empty. By
eleven on a Saturday the visitor lot behind Warehouse 21 is full and you are
walking in from Street 8, which is three minutes but feels longer in June.

Come at half eight, park outside, and the rest of the day works.

## 8:45am — breakfast while the room is quiet

I am obviously not impartial. But the practical point stands: the galleries do
not open until ten, so the first ninety minutes of the Avenue are for eating.

The long table is at its best now — it is genuinely communal until about noon and
you will end up talking to whoever is next to you, which on a Saturday morning is
usually someone who has also come early for the parking.

Order the shakshuka, or the kunafa french toast if you are going to be walking
for four hours.

## 10:00am — the galleries, in the right order

Most of the Avenue's spaces open at ten and close at seven, and almost all of
them shut on Sundays. That one fact catches out more visitors than anything else.

Start at the far end and work back towards the entrance. The reason is light:
the western spaces get direct sun through their roof lights in the morning and
are noticeably better before noon, while the eastern warehouses are consistent
all day.

<Note title="Check before you build a day around it">

Between exhibitions, several spaces close entirely for install — sometimes for
two weeks. The Alserkal Avenue programme page is kept current; the individual
gallery instagrams are more current still.

</Note>

## 12:00pm — Concrete

The OMA-designed exhibition hall in the middle of the Avenue. Whatever is on,
put your head in — the building is worth ten minutes on its own, and the movable
walls mean it is a different room every time.

The light through the polycarbonate façade is best in the late afternoon, around
four, if you are choosing a time to photograph it.

## 1:30pm — the second coffee, and a decision

You are now at the point where most people either flag or commit.

If you are flagging: the Avenue has shade, seating and air conditioning, and
there is no shame in a two-hour lunch.

If you are committing: The Courtyard on Street 4a is a six-minute drive. It is
the older, scruffier arts complex — less curated, more workshops and framers than
white-cube galleries, and worth it for the contrast.

## 4:00pm — the part nobody plans for

Between four and six the light in Al Quoz does something specific. The warehouses
are low, the streets run roughly east-west, and the sun comes down the length of
them.

If you only do one thing from this guide that is not eating, walk the length of
17th Street at five o'clock between November and March.

## 6:00pm — how to leave

Taxis do not cruise Al Quoz. Book one, or walk out to Al Manara Street where they
do. Allow fifteen minutes more than you think on a Saturday evening.

Equiti Metro on the Red Line is 2.4km away, which is a seven-minute taxi and not
a walk you want between May and September. In winter it is pleasant. In July it
is a genuinely bad idea and people try it every year.

## The compressed version

Park before nine. Eat first. Galleries from ten, far end backwards. Concrete at
midday. Walk 17th Street at five. Book your taxi before you want it.

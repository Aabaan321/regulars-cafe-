Dubai taught a generation of people that brunch means unlimited. Four hours, a
hundred dishes, a queue at the carving station, and a bill that only makes sense
if you eat until you regret it.

I am not going to tell you that is wrong. I have had a very good time at those.

But it did something to how people order in a room like ours, where brunch means
a kitchen with four burners and a menu of eleven plates. People arrive braced for
volume and order like they are stocking a buffet — three large plates for two
people, all of them rich, all arriving at once, half of them going back cold.

So, a short and opinionated guide to ordering three good plates.

## One: order in waves, not all at once

The single biggest improvement you can make. Order two things. Eat them. Then
decide.

A kitchen our size fires to order, which means your second wave arrives eight to
twelve minutes after you ask for it — hot, and after you have made room. Ordering
everything up front guarantees that at least one plate sits under a heat lamp
that we do not own.

Nobody will rush you. The turn time is ninety minutes and we have never once
enforced it on a table that was still eating.

## Two: one rich, one sharp, one green

The most common mistake is three rich plates. Truffle mushrooms, kunafa french
toast and a short rib hash is three excellent dishes that fight each other.

The shape that works:

- **Something rich.** Truffle mushrooms on toast, the short rib hash, the kunafa
  french toast if you are having it as a main rather than a dessert.
- **Something sharp.** Foul medames with the lemon squeezed in properly, or the
  chilli scrambled eggs. Acid and heat reset the palate.
- **Something green.** The green bowl, or just the leaf salad off the omelette.
  It sounds like health advice. It is actually about being able to taste the
  third plate as clearly as the first.

## Three: the shakshuka is a sharing dish and we will not say so

It arrives in the pan it was cooked in with enough bread for two. It is on the
breakfast menu at AED 62 and it is the best value on the page. If there are two
of you and you order one shakshuka, one plate of foul, and a side of halloumi,
you will eat well for under a hundred dirhams between you.

## Four: order the coffee after the food, not with it

This is the one nobody does.

If you order a flat white with your food, it arrives with your food, and then
sits going lukewarm while you eat eggs. Order water with the food and coffee when
the plates go. You get it at the temperature it was made for, and the barista
gets to make it properly instead of racing the kitchen.

If you want caffeine immediately — and fair enough — order an espresso or a
cortado on arrival. Small, fast, finished before the food lands.

## Five: tell us the truth about allergies

Not because of liability. Because *"a bit of a dairy thing"* and *"anaphylactic
to dairy"* lead to completely different behaviour in the kitchen, and we would
rather run the second protocol than guess.

Our kitchen handles gluten, nuts, sesame and dairy in one room. We cannot promise
zero cross-contact and we will tell you so rather than reassure you. What we can
do is change a pan, change a board, and have one person cook your plate.
